Details: measurements, definitions, and sources

Measurements for the current tracing

Definitions

For sinus rhythm, the normal heart-rate range is 60–100 bpm; above 100 is sinus tachycardia and below 60 is sinus bradycardia. The normal PR interval is 0.12–0.20 s; when it exceeds 0.20 s on every beat, it is first-degree AV block. QRS duration is normally about 0.06–0.10 s; at 0.12 s or wider, consider bundle branch block and similar conditions. In atrial fibrillation there are no P waves and the RR intervals are irregular.

About the PR interval slider

The slider works only with the normal sinus rhythm template. The P-wave portion is cut out of the median beat of the real recording and repositioned relative to the QRS. The P-wave shape itself is exactly as recorded — it is not a redrawn waveform. For the other templates, the PR interval measured on the real recording is shown as-is (the slider has no effect). Continuous-strip templates (AV block patterns, ventricular tachycardia, etc.) play the recording exactly as recorded, and the heart-rate slider has no effect either.

Why there is no continuous QRS-width control

Changing QRS width continuously with a slider would require interpolating between real recordings, and an interpolated waveform would be one that never existed. Instead, different QRS widths are shown by switching to real-recording templates such as the bundle branch blocks .

Each template is either a median beat built from all beats of a recording (aligned on lead II) or a continuous segment of the recording itself. PR interval, QRS duration, and QT are measured automatically on the median beat (QRS duration is measured from onset to offset across all 12 leads). Where the end of the T wave could not be detected reliably, QT is shown as "–" rather than risk a wrong number. For the complete heart block strip, automated detection alone was not sufficient to verify it, so a recording in which P waves visibly march through independently of the QRS was selected by visual inspection. Definitions follow the AHA/ACCF/HRS recommendations for the standardization and interpretation of the electrocardiogram (Kligfield P, et al. 2009) and the Japanese Circulation Society guideline on diagnosis and risk assessment of arrhythmias (2022 revision).

Median-beat templates repeat one representative beat from a real recording. Changing the heart rate changes only the interval between beats (the RR interval); the shape of each beat stays exactly as recorded. For atrial fibrillation and sinus arrhythmia, the intervals are made irregular to match the RR variability measured in the original recording. Continuous-strip templates loop a segment of the real recording as-is.
